Analytical Generalized Impulse Responses and Connectedness in Score-Driven Models

Emilija Dzuverovic · 2026

Download paper (PDF)  ·  Slides (PDF)

Abstract

We derive analytical generalized impulse responses and construct connectedness measures for a nonlinear score-driven model for strictly positive variables. The model is based on the flexible multivariate generalized beta of the second kind (MGB2) distribution, which accommodates skewness and heavy tails while encompassing several familiar distributions as special or limiting cases. We establish conditions governing shock transmission across series and horizons and explain how market conditions affect connectedness through baseline forecasts. Simulations demonstrate that similar levels of cumulative connectedness can conceal substantially different patterns of dynamic transmission. We apply our framework to daily realized variances of exchange rates and futures. For exchange rates, total connectedness including impact and the following ten trading days is about 40% and nearly constant over time, while impact responses are 2.5 to 4.5 times as large on a selected high-volatility date as on a selected low-volatility date. In the application to four futures series, over the ten trading days after the shock, we identify the S&P 500 E-mini as the largest net transmitter of volatility, followed by crude oil, while natural gas and gold are net receivers. The empirical results underscore the importance of reporting response magnitudes alongside connectedness measures and distinguishing responses on impact from those at later horizons.
